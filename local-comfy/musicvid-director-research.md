# Music-video directing craft: research for a house style (2026-10-31)

## Part A: Director by director (concrete practice only)

**Michel Gondry**
- **Concept:** He maps each musical element to one type of visual element. In Daft Punk's "Around the World", each group of performers is one instrument: robots are the talk-box voice, athletes the bass, mummies the drum machine. Sources disagree on which instrument the skeletons and swimmers are (https://signalvnoise.com/archives2/michel_gondrys_happy_accidents, https://www.songfacts.com/facts/daft-punk/around-the-world).
- **Repetition:** In "Star Guitar", "the disposition of each passing element of the landscape is positioned in sync with the music." Each repeated sound returns as a repeated object, such as water towers or smokestacks (https://laughingsquid.com/how-michel-gondry-uses-repetition-in-music-videos/).
- **Planning:** "The less you plan, the more you get." He leaves "room for randomness and happy accidents" (signalvnoise, above).
- **Technique:** He keeps the effects in-camera and wants texture: "I want the image to be shot through layers of glass and texture." He says back projection gives him "something that's really organically incorporated, which you can never get with blue screen" (https://www.dga.org/craft/dgaq/issues/0601-spring-2006/independent-voice-michel-gondry).
- **Rule:** One system maps sound to image. The repeats in the song become repeats on screen.

**Spike Jonze**
- **Concept:** He uses a fiction or genre frame and commits to it. "Praise You" invents a bad amateur dance troupe, the "Torrance Community Dance Group", filmed guerrilla-style outside Grauman's Chinese Theater (https://www.wral.com/archive/17704400/, https://www.creativereview.co.uk/oral-history-praise-you-fatboy-slim/). The Creative Review oral history returned 403, so the troupe detail comes from secondary coverage.
- **Genre pastiche:** "Sabotage" is cut as the opening credits of a fake 1970s cop show. The crew fit in one van and shot with no permits (https://www.mentalfloss.com/article/604049/beastie-boys-sabotage-music-video-facts).
- **Weapon of Choice:** The treatment was "really simple." It is one performer and one fantasy, shot in long tracking takes (radiox.co.uk, https://radiox.co.uk/features/x-plained/did-christopher-walken-really-dance-in-weapon-of-choice).
- **Tone:** "I never feel like I'm making fun of any character."
- **Rule:** Pick a frame (a fake troupe, a fake TV show) and play it completely straight.

**Hype Williams**
- **Signature:** A fisheye or ultrawide lens, a low vantage point, and the performer centred and close to the lens. Sources say it gives "even the most realistic surroundings a cartoonish pop." He also uses saturated colour, split screens and slow motion (https://www.artforum.com/columns/sasha-geffen-on-willow-and-olivia-rodrigo-250233/, https://thecolrgirl.substack.com/p/the-colors-of-hype-wlliams).
- **Rule:** The performer plays to the lens. The lens exaggerates the performer.
- **Caveat:** I found only critical descriptions, no first-person method from Williams.

**Mark Romanek**
- **"Hurt":** The idea was to frame the 70-year-old Cash with "no gimmicks and no trick shots" (https://www.mtv.com/news/6veiwo/johnny-cashs-hurt-delves-into-life-of-former-hell-raiser-vma-lens-recap).
- **Structure in the edit:** The archival montage was found in post: "the concept completely changed in the edit" (https://www.provideocoalition.com/hurt_editor/amp/). That quote is the author's summary of the DVD, not Romanek's words.
- **Range:** He also made the maximal "Scream." He picks the register that suits the song.
- **Rule:** Restraint is a valid concept. A still face that sings straight to the camera can carry a whole video.

**David Fincher**
- **"Vogue":** Black-and-white Golden Age glamour lighting with posed tableaux. Madonna: "David Fincher told me to stand and hit different poses with my body and face" (https://www.songfacts.com/facts/madonna/vogue).
- **Period reference:** One source ties the lighting to 1930s Horst photographs. I could not confirm a Hurrell link (https://www.anothermag.com/fashion-beauty/8796/five-of-the-worlds-most-fashionable-music-videos).
- **"Freedom! '90":** Supermodels lip-sync in place of the absent star (https://www.biography.com/news/george-michael-freedom-90-song-music-video).
- **Rule:** One shot, one pose, one hard light. Lip-sync can go to anyone except the singer.

**Jonathan Glazer**
- **"Virtual Insanity":** One impossible-space gag. The walls and ceiling roll on wheels with the camera locked to them, so the floor appears to move (https://kottke.org/22/12/how-jamiroquai-shot-their-iconic-virtual-insanity-video, https://www.killscreen.com/recreate-jamiroquais-virtual-insanity-music-video-this-game/).
- **"Karma Police":** He tried to make the camera "as subjective as possible" and felt he "missed emotionally." He calls the companion video, "Rabbit in Your Headlights", the better execution (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabbit_in_Your_Headlights). Spin quotes him calling a video "like an equation," but only as a fragment (https://www.spinmagazine.com/2005/09/prime-directives/).
- **Rule:** Find one physical illusion, then build the whole video on it.

**Chris Cunningham**
- **Frame-level sync:** On "Rubber Johnny" he says the work was nearer to animation than editing. He built it two frames at a time and sometimes spent a full day to lock a few frames to the music (https://filmmakermagazine.com/?p=1948). That page returned 403, so this comes from the search summary only.
- **Rule:** On percussive music, the image hits the transient, not the general beat.
- **Caveat:** I found no first-person account of his sync method on "Come to Daddy" or "Windowlicker."

**Hiro Murai** ("This Is America", with cinematographer Larkin Seiple)
- **Construction:** Six long takes, "camera choreography on top of the actual choreography" (https://www.kodak.com/en/motion/blog-post/hiro-murai-larkin-seiple/, https://www.premiumbeat.com/blog/interview-editor-this-is-america/).
- **Camera:** "We wanted the camera to be constantly drifting," and the camera move "had to match the actors" (Seiple, Kodak).
- **Framing:** Stay wide enough that "the background is [not] mush." The ending is meant to land as a portrait, "from a chaotic environment to quiet" (Kodak).
- **Premise:** "a really crazy confluence of tone changes." He says there is "Looney Tunes logic in there somewhere" (https://thefader.com/2018/05/11/this-is-america-director-hiro-murai-music-video-interview).
- **Rule:** The camera follows the body. Background action carries meaning. End on a still portrait.

**Dave Meyers**
- **Source of ideas:** "The script is somewhat dictated to me by the artist and the music."
- **Lyrics:** "If it's in the lyrics, it's permission for me to do something crazy."
- **Literalism:** He tries to avoid "see say" literalism (https://daily.redbullmusicacademy.com/2015/07/dave-meyers-interview).
- **Rule:** A lyric is a licence for a visual gag. It is not a caption to illustrate.

**Floria Sigismondi**
- **Look:** German Expressionist tableaux, "aggressive, choppy cuts," and jittery, dilating camerawork (https://floodmagazine.com/74500/floria-sigismondi-goddess-of-grotesquery/, https://lbbonline.com/news/into-the-library-with-floria-sigismondi).
- **Gap:** I could not find her shot-level method.

**Anton Corbijn**
- **"Enjoy the Silence":** One emblematic image, the singer as a king who carries a deckchair through empty landscapes, shot partly on Super-8 (https://www.dmlive.wiki/wiki/Enjoy_The_Silence_(official_music_video)).
- **Approach:** Scholars call his work photographic "staged atmospheres" (https://pure.qub.ac.uk/en/publications/ienjoy-the-silencei-anton-corbijn-and-the-synesthetic-representat/).
- **Rule:** One iconic figure with one prop, repeated across many locations.

**Melina Matsoukas**
- **Method:** Research first. "Formation" draws on Morrison, Angelou and Butler, and on Black protest and parade imagery. It is built as symbolic tableaux, such as the singer on a sinking police car (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melina_Matsoukas, https://www.essence.com/entertainment/melina-matsoukas-beyonce-formation-secrets).
- **Rule:** Every image is a researched symbol, and each symbol gets its own tableau shot.

**Daniels (Kwan and Scheinert)**
- **"Turn Down for What":** One absurd premise, a man who physically cannot turn down, escalated until the video is "pretty relentless."
- **Method:** No storyboards, so they are not locked into "preconceived ideas." "Almost everything in the video is practical." Their advice: "come up with ideas that are going to look good," and shoot what you know you can pull off (https://nofilmschool.com/daniels-music-video).
- **Rule:** One premise, escalated every section.

**Busby Berkeley**
- **His words:** "It is the camera that must dance" (quoted at https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Busby_Berkeley).
- **One camera:** Sources say he used one camera, not the usual four, so no one could re-cut his numbers (https://www.tcm.com/articles/021599/special-theme-busby-berkeley). Other sources say many numbers were shot with several cameras, so this is contested.
- **The top shot:** The camera looks straight down at 90° and turns dancers into kaleidoscope geometry.
- **Parade of faces:** One loving close-up of each chorus girl, cut in series (https://cinemascholars.com/busby-berkeley-tortured-genius/).
- **Not verified:** The line "the camera's eye was the audience's eye" is EBSCO's wording, not Berkeley's (https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/film/forty-second-street-defines-1930s-film-musicals).

**Fleischer Studios** (Betty Boop and the Cab Calloway shorts)
- **Rotoscope:** Calloway's real dance is traced onto a ghost walrus "with serpentine hips" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnie_the_Moocher_(film)).
- **Transformations:** Objects and bodies turn into other things, for example a father's head becomes a phonograph.
- **Motion:** Characters "are constantly bobbing up and down to the beat and any prop or body part can be turned into a musical instrument" (https://www.cartoonbrew.com/cartoon-study/the-rubber-hose-classics-that-inspired-the-cuphead-show-213700.html).
- **Lyric cue:** The bouncing ball, patented in 1926, puts the lyric on screen (https://www.atos.org/node/2787).
- **Sound:** The studio usually recorded voices after the animation and let actors ad-lib. Disney recorded first (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleischer_Studios). The sources for this are thin.

## Part B: Synthesis

### 1. Shared principles, ranked by how often they appear (out of 15)

1. **One governing idea or rule (about 11 of 15):** Gondry, Jonze, Glazer, Corbijn, Daniels, Romanek, Fincher, Berkeley, Matsoukas, Hype Williams and Fleischer. You can say the concept in one sentence, and every shot obeys it.
2. **Visual structure follows musical structure (about 8):** Gondry, Berkeley, Fleischer, Cunningham, Meyers, Murai, Romanek and Daniels. Verses, choruses, loops and hits each get a visual counterpart. A repeated sound brings back a repeated image.
3. **Repetition, then escalation (about 7):** Gondry, Daniels, Berkeley, Fleischer, Corbijn, Murai and Jonze. Set up a pattern, return to it, and make each return bigger or stranger.
4. **The performer plays to the camera (about 7):** Hype Williams, Fincher, Romanek, Murai's closing portrait, Berkeley's parade of faces, Corbijn and Matsoukas. The key moments are frontal, centred and direct.
5. **The camera serves the body (about 5):** Berkeley, Murai/Seiple, Jonze, Gondry and Sigismondi. The camera moves because the performer moves, never on its own.
6. **Practical effects and constraints become the style (about 5):** Gondry, Glazer, Daniels, Jonze and Fleischer.
7. **Genre or period pastiche played straight (about 4):** Jonze, Fincher, Matsoukas and Corbijn.
8. **A tonal turn late in the video (about 3):** Murai, Romanek and Fleischer's chase.

**Cut timing:** I found very little first-person material on exact cut timing. Cunningham locks to transients, and Gondry syncs objects to loops. A "cut on the phrase" rule is a fair inference from structural mapping, but no source states it outright.

### 2. Shot-level checklist (every shot must specify these)

1. **Lyric span:** The exact words and the bar or beat range. Start the shot on a downbeat or the first beat of a phrase.
2. **Single action beat:** One verb, such as "points on 'Minnie'" or "kicks on the snare hit."
3. **Framing:** Wide, medium, close-up or extreme close-up. Note what stays readable in the background (Seiple's "not mush" rule).
4. **Angle:** Eye level, low (Williams), top shot (Berkeley), or theatre-seat view.
5. **Camera move:** Static, a slow push, a drift that follows the body, or none. Give a reason tied to what the performer does.
6. **Performer's relation to camera:** Direct address, profile or turned away. Name the moment the performer looks into the lens.
7. **Prop and how it is used:** What it is, and how it becomes an instrument or transforms (Fleischer).
8. **Pattern role:** Does the shot set up a motif, repeat it, or escalate it? Say which earlier shot it echoes.
9. **Lip-sync or not:** Is this shot singing, reaction, or a cutaway?
10. **Exit state:** The last frame, so the next cut can match on pose or action.

### 3. Techniques for 2–5 second generated clips of 1930s cartoon singers

Each clip is animated from one still, the camera is unreliable, and no shot runs past about 5 seconds.

**Use these:**
- **Let the character move, not the camera.** Turn Berkeley's "camera must dance" around: the camera is unreliable, so the character dances. Ask for a static or locked-off proscenium frame, then put rubber-hose bob-to-the-beat motion into the character.
- **Use the cut as the camera move.** You cannot trust a push-in inside one clip, so build it across clips: wide, then medium, then close-up across one phrase.
- **One clip, one lyric phrase, one action beat.** Two to five seconds is about one bar to one phrase at vaudeville tempos. Cut on the phrase boundary, never in the middle of a word.
- **Gondry's mapping, applied to the stage:** Give each recurring musical element a fixed visual. For example, horn stabs always cut to the band, and the chorus always returns to the same front-on medium shot. You get repetition for free, and continuity errors matter less.
- **Escalate at each chorus return.** Use the same framing with a bigger action, an extra prop, or a transformation. This is what Daniels and Fleischer do.
- **Fleischer transformations as cut points.** A prop that becomes an instrument, or a face that becomes an object, is a self-contained 2–3 s event. Line it up with a hit or the end of a lyric.
- **Parade of faces.** Use a series of 1.5–2.5 s close-ups of chorus members or audience animals, each with one take or wink. It is cheap, it reads well, and it avoids the hard lip-sync problem.
- **Top shot as a still kaleidoscope.** A symmetric overhead still with a slow rotation or a breathing scale-pulse needs almost no camera control.
- **Fincher-style poses.** Each clip starts and ends on a strong silhouette pose. Make the source still the downbeat pose, so the animation only needs to get from one pose to the next.
- **Hype Williams style low-angle close-up** for the big sustained notes and the singer's money moments. Wide, short faces give the generator big, simple mouth shapes.
- **Put lip-sync where it reads.** Put sustained, open-vowel notes in close-ups. Put fast patter in wide or medium shots or cutaways (band, audience, props), where small mouth errors do not show.
- **One emblematic prop, Corbijn-style:** a cane, top hat or megaphone carried through every scene. It holds the video together across clips that were generated separately.

**Avoid these:**
- Murai and Jonze long takes and drifting one-shots. You cannot sustain them in 5 seconds or keep them continuous across clips.
- Glazer-style spatial illusions, which need precise camera control.
- Cunningham frame-locked hits. They are only possible later, as edit-level retiming, not as generation.

**Fake continuity instead:** Match-cut on pose or action, and keep stage geography fixed: the proscenium, footlights, curtain colour, and a stage-left/stage-right rule.

## Gaps I could not verify

- First-person shot method from Hype Williams, Sigismondi, Corbijn, Matsoukas or Fincher. Only secondary descriptions turned up.
- Murai's New York Times interview, which is the primary source on his blocking. I could not access it.
- Pages that returned 403: the Creative Review "Praise You" oral history and the Filmmaker Magazine Cunningham piece. Those claims come from search summaries.
- Berkeley's single camera is contested. The "audience's eye" line is not his.
- The exact instrument mapping in "Around the World" varies between sources.
- How Fleischer animators timed the animation to music. I found no primary documents.
- Any explicit director quote that says "cut on the phrase" rather than on the beat.
