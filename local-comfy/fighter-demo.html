Seth and Cadbury, playable

Two characters, seven moves each. Cadbury's sheets came out of the same pipeline with no tuning changes — same constants, same packer — which was the real test. A MiniMax H3 clip supplies the motion; every cell it chooses is then repainted as a drawing by Qwen-Image-Edit before packing, so nothing that ships is a video frame. All on gpu-worker, no cloud credits. Click the screen, then use the keys.

How a move gets made

Top to bottom in time. The clip is a pose source, not artwork — it is the only thing that knows how a punch moves, and the only thing that cannot draw.

1 · Render

gpu-worker · ComfyUI · ~170s per move · hires_sprite.py

1The character's reference plate One nano-banana original. It is the identity reference for every move, so the character cannot drift between them.
2Stage lock + move recipe SPRITE_LOCK fixes the flat magenta screen, the locked-off side view and the ground line; the recipe adds only the movement. Anything that varies here reads later as the camera moving.
3MiniMax H3, 832² · 20 steps · 61 frames Frame 1 is pinned to a magenta plate so the clip starts on the stage. At 832 he fills the frame at ~600px, which is why the packer scales down.
↓ an .mp4 on a magenta screen — 61 frames of motion

2 · Choose & repaint

CPU picks, GPU redraws · ~30s per cell · repaint_cells.py

4Pick the cells, not every frame Finds the gait period by self-similarity, keeps the pose extremes where motion energy peaks, then nudges each pick to the sharpest frame within two either side. Choosing has to happen on the video, because only the sequence knows where the motion is. 6 to 10 cells survive out of 61 frames.
5Repaint each cell as a drawing Each chosen frame goes back through Qwen-Image-Edit as an edit of itself — "this is a repaint, the pose is already correct". That distinction is the whole trick: hand the same frame over as a reference image and Qwen re-poses the character, because a reference is an identity reference. Measured edge sharpness 8.8 → 14.9, and one locked seed keeps the drawing from wandering between cells.
↓ clean drawings on flat magenta

3 · Pack

CPU · seconds · sprite_sheet.py

6Key the magenta out Soft distance ramp against the screen colour measured from the corners, plus a despill on blue-above-green. The de-noising pass the video frames needed is switched off here — a repaint has no codec noise, and the median only cost halftone dots.
7Anchor on the feet Bottom centre of each silhouette, median-smoothed over three cells. This is the origin a game engine expects, and it is what stops him swimming in the cell.
8One scale for all seven moves Measures every move first, then packs them all at the same pixels-per-character — against the tallest silhouette and the highest reach, because the feet line sits at 94% of the cell, so fitting the cell is not the same as fitting above the feet.
9Emit the atlas seth-walk.png + .json + a ready-made CSS steps() animation. Uniform cells, transparent background, interchangeable.
↓ one atlas + seth.json

4 · Play

this page · <canvas>, no engine

10The sheet describes itself The page reads the flat frame list and its named tags — fps, loop, direction, hold_key, per-frame hold and pivot — straight out of the JSON, so a repack never leaves it stale.
11State machine Loops (idle, walk) advance freely; one-shots lock the character until the last cell plays. Hold moves then stay on that cell while the key is down — snapping straight to it is what made crouch flash a single frame.
12Draw Canvas backing store matched to device pixels, cell drawn with its pivot on the floor line, and the sprite mirrored for facing. Every extra resample here costs crispness the packer already paid for.
Two failed ideas worth not repeating. Refining an H3 frame with partial denoise makes mush, because the Lightning LoRA needs cfg 1.0 and a full denoise. And feeding an H3 frame to Qwen as a reference image comes back crisp but re-posed — a reference image is an identity reference, not a pose reference. Editing the frame in place is the version that works, and it was the last thing tried rather than the first.

Render size still mattered. The original 512×512 / 10-step clips drew the character about 240px tall in a soft frame; at 832×832 and 20 steps he fills it at roughly 600px, which is enough for the cell picker to tell a sharp frame from a blurred one. The repaint fixes the drawing, not the choosing.
